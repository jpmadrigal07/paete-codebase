Paete, Laguna: the Carving Capital of the Philippines

The Carving Capital of the Philippines, named after the paet, the carver's chisel. A town of woodcarvers, taka makers, a 17th-century stone church, and three crosses on the mountain above it.

Ukit · Wood carvingCarved by hand, for four centuriesUkit is the Tagalog word for carving.

Paete's name comes from paet, the chisel. Carving is the town's oldest trade and still its best known: santos for churches and home altars, relief panels, furniture, and ornaments, worked by families who pass the craft down.

Work starts with the outline, then the background is cut away so the design stands up in relief. Fine lines like petal veins go in last.

Known for
Santos (religious images), relief panels, furniture, ornaments

Taka · Papier-mâchéPaper shaped over a carvingEvery taka starts as a wooden sculpture.

A carver makes a wooden form, the takaan. It is coated with wax or starch paste, covered in layers of brown paper or newsprint, and dried in the sun. Then the shell is slit open, the wooden form comes out to be used again, and the halves are joined and painted in bright colors.

Horses, carabaos and Maria Clara dolls are the classics, with Santa Claus and reindeer for Christmas. The first known taka is credited to Maria Bague in the 1920s. Production peaked in the mid-1970s and early 1980s.

Made from
Brown paper or newsprint, paste, a carved wooden mold
Classic forms
Horse, carabao, Maria Clara doll

Simbahan · St. James the Apostle ParishStone, earthquakes and a painted Heaven and HellSaint James the Apostle Parish Church, also called the Church of Paete.

Franciscans founded the parish in 1580, and the first stone church went up in 1646. Earthquakes have damaged or brought it down several times, including in 1717, 1880 and 1937, and each time it was rebuilt. The church is Baroque, built of volcanic tuff, with large buttresses along its sides to help it stand up to quakes.

Inside are José Luciano Dans's murals Langit, Lupa at Impiyerno (Heaven, Earth and Hell), painted with natural pigments mixed with volcanic ash, along with two San Cristobal paintings on wood panels and a Last Judgment mural from around 1720.

Patron
St. James the Apostle (Santiago Apostol)
Fiesta
July 25
Heritage
National Historical Landmark; National Cultural Treasure (2015)

Tatlong Krus · Mount HumarapA thousand steps to three crossesTatlong Krus means "three crosses."

Three crosses stand on the summit of Mount Humarap, a peak of the Sierra Madre above the town. They began as wooden crosses and are now concrete. During Holy Week, hundreds of pilgrims climb to pray and remember the suffering of Christ.

Hikers count about 300 steps to a fork, where a side trail leads to Matabungka Falls, then about 700 more to the top. It is an easy climb of roughly 45 minutes, from the edge of town to about 315 m above the lake. From the top you look out over Paete, Laguna de Bay and Mt. Sembrano across the water. The center cross carries a bronze figure of Christ.
